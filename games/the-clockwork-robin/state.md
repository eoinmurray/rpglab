# The Clockwork Robin

## Opening

You wake in darkness to three hard knocks at your door.

## Status

Ongoing.

## Current scene

You, Ivy, and Mrs Alder are at the inn after you explained that Bea has the missing robin at Willow Cottage and intends to return it safely. Mrs Alder is still troubled by the unseen note but has agreed to hear Bea’s explanation rather than accuse her. Ivy has suggested looking again at the reading-room mantel. Rain continues, and the spring fair opens after breakfast.

## World in motion

Downstairs at the inn, lamps glow around the empty display where Mrs Alder’s mechanical exhibits were meant to perform. Bea remains at Willow Cottage with the wrapped robin and the older woman. The note Bea says she left has not yet been found. Mrs Alder now knows the robin is safe and is willing to resolve the misunderstanding.

## Characters

### Anomancer

The player. A guest at the inn, free to decide what matters and how to act.

### Ivy Rook

The player’s travelling companion, a warm, dry-witted amateur naturalist with a battered sketchbook and a weakness for other people’s biscuits. Ivy notices physical details, offers opinions, and acts like a real companion, but never chooses the player’s conclusions or course. She is with the player at the inn.

### Mrs Alder

The village clockmaker and keeper of the fair’s mechanical exhibits. She now knows the robin is safe and will hear Bea’s explanation, though she remains concerned that the note never reached her.

### Bea Lark

The village florist. She is at Willow Cottage with the robin and an older woman. She believed she had permission, intends to return the robin, and needs to explain the misunderstanding.

### Theo Wren

Mrs Alder’s well-meaning apprentice. At the clock shop. He speaks loosely when nervous and assumes everyone understands what he meant.

## Active arcs

### The missing robin

Phase: resolution and clarification. The robin has been found safe at Willow Cottage with Bea. Bea has promised to return it and explain. Mrs Alder now knows it is safe and is willing to hear her out. The note remains unresolved.

### Ivy and Anomancer

Phase: companionship. Ivy is participating naturally in the investigation.

### The spring fair

Phase: approaching. The fair creates gentle social pressure and movement around the village. It must never become a catastrophe clock.

## Open possibilities

The player may reasonably suspect a misunderstanding, sentimental borrowing, attempted repair, prank, or theft. The older woman’s identity and connection to the tune remain open.

## Secrets

### What happened before play

The clockwork robin plays a tune called “First Primroses.” Bea Lark’s mother, Ruth, taught that tune to Mrs Alder’s late husband, who built the robin around its music cylinder. Ruth’s memory is failing, and she is leaving the village after the fair to live with Bea’s sister.

Yesterday Bea asked Theo whether Mrs Alder would mind Ruth hearing the robin once more. Theo said, “She never minds it being played,” meaning inside the reading room. Bea understood this as permission to borrow it briefly.

Before dawn, while arranging fair flowers at the Rosehip Inn, Bea wrapped the robin in green florist’s felt and carried it to Willow Cottage. She left Mrs Alder a note on the reading-room mantel. A gust from an open window blew the note behind the radiator before anyone read it. Loose primrose pollen and one green wool thread remain near the empty display stand.

Bea intends to return the robin before breakfast. She believes she has permission and has no wish to steal, damage, or conceal it beyond keeping the visit quiet for Ruth’s dignity.

### Current hidden situation

The robin is safe at Willow Cottage with Bea and Ruth. Bea has finished the listening and stopped the robin, but is waiting for the next move before carrying it back or explaining everything herself. Mrs Alder now knows where the robin is and is prepared to hear Bea’s explanation.

### Revelation status

The missing object: revealed. Physical traces of yellow pollen and green wool: revealed. The matching courtyard fiber and likely route toward the cottages: revealed. The robin’s presence at Willow Cottage and Bea’s involvement: revealed. A clockwork melody is publicly audible and its source is confirmed as the robin. Bea’s claim that she left Mrs Alder a note is revealed; the note’s status, Theo’s ambiguous permission, the older woman’s identity, and her connection to the tune remain hidden.

## Tone and direction

Keep this a warm, lightly comic village mystery with rain, clocks, flowers, tea, and modest human stakes. No violence, supernatural threat, cruelty, villainous conspiracy, or false urgency. Reveal at most one significant fact per turn. Preserve the secret history while allowing the investigation, relationships, and resolution to evolve freely.
